# The Adoption Chain
### Three decisions made before go-live that determine everything after

**Domain:** Data SaaS · Implementation & Onboarding  
**Created:** March 2026  
**Author:** Priyanka Ranganathan

---

## The Core Insight

Adoption doesn't fail at go-live. It fails because of three decisions made in the months before it.

In data SaaS, the software only works when the customer's data works. That means onboarding isn't a support function — **it's the product itself.**

You can't fix adoption after go-live. The decisions that determine it were made months before.

## The Three Decisions

### Decision 01 — End users are excluded
**The assumption:** *"The senior stakeholder represents the team."*

The people who will use the tool every day are never consulted during implementation. A senior stakeholder represents them — but that person only uses one part of the tool. End users show up on go-live day to a product built without their input, their workflow, or their reality in mind.

**What happens:**
- Tool is configured for the buyer, not the daily user
- End users see the product for the first time at go-live
- Zero investment — they never shaped it, so they don't own it

**What to do instead:**
- Involve end users once in the early stage — inform, ask, listen
- Map every user role and what specific insight they need from the product
- Do not rely on a senior stakeholder to represent everyone below them

---

### Decision 02 — Data quality is deferred
**The assumption:** *"We'll clean it up as we go."*

Real data problems only surface after go-live. Only after the data is in the system do you see the real problems — wrong formats, missing fields, inconsistent naming, duplicate records. By then, dashboards are populated with bad data and the insights are wrong.

**What happens:**
- Real data problems surface post go-live when support is being reduced
- Dashboards populate with bad data — insights are meaningless or wrong
- Trust collapses in the first two weeks
- Users lose motivation during the 2–3 week fix period post go-live

**What to do instead:**
- Validate data quality before any configuration begins
- Define "good enough data" as a hard go-live criterion — not a nice-to-have
- Run a data audit in week one of the project, not week ten

---

### Decision 03 — Go-live is treated as the finish line
**The assumption:** *"Hyper care is two months. Then they're on their own."*

Post go-live support is typically two months, minimal, and withdrawn exactly when the real problems are surfacing. Technically, the project begins at go-live — that is when real usage starts, real issues emerge, and real adoption either takes root or dies.

**What happens:**
- Support withdraws exactly when real problems emerge
- One champion uses the product — everyone else quietly stops
- At renewal: "do we actually use this?" — contract cancelled
- The team that ran the 12-month implementation is gone before adoption is real

**What to do instead:**
- Treat go-live as day one of the real implementation, not the end
- Run a structured 30-day adoption review with the customer
- Identify quiet users before renewal — not after cancellation
- Build internal champions: give the power user a shareable report to show colleagues the value

## The Chain

```
End users excluded  →  Data quality deferred  →  Go-live = finish line
       ↓                        ↓                         ↓
Tool built for               Insights are              Nobody there
the wrong person             meaningless               when it matters
       ↓                        ↓                         ↓
                    ADOPTION FAILS
```

These are not three independent failures. They are one chain. Each decision makes the next failure more likely. Fix the first one and the others become easier to solve.
