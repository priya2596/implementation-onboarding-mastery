---

> Churn decisions are made weeks before the call. By the time a customer says they are leaving, they have already built a story in their head. Most CSMs arrive unprepared with a discount or a defence. Neither works.
>
> This playbook is about showing up differently.

---

## The mindset shift

You are not going into this call to save the account in the room. That ship has likely sailed.

You are going in to break the story they have already built, the one that says nobody was paying attention, nobody cared, they were just a contract number.

The only way to break that story is to show evidence that contradicts it. Evidence that you noticed. Evidence that you did the work before you showed up.

That starts with acknowledgement before questions, before solutions, before roadmap slides. Nobody who is frustrated wants to be defended at or questioned first. They want to feel that their experience was real and that someone finally recognises it.

**Acknowledge first. Evidence second. Questions third.**

### GRR and NRR as your early warning system

**GRR (Gross Revenue Retention)** tells you the base is leaking. When GRR drops, revenue is leaving through churn or downgrades from your existing customers. It does not count expansions. The ceiling is 100%. When it falls, something is wrong.

**NRR (Net Revenue Retention)** gives you the fuller picture. When NRR falls below 100%, your existing customer base is shrinking net of all expansions. One account growing cannot hide others quietly leaving.

**The critical nuance:** Both GRR and NRR are lagging indicators. By the time they move, the churn decision is already forming in the customer's head. The metrics tell you to go looking. They do not tell you who or why.

### Account-level leading indicators

These signals appear weeks or months before the churn conversation. A CSM watching these is never surprised.

**Engagement signals**
- Login frequency dropping — fewer users logging in, or the same users logging in less often
- Session length shortening — they are opening the product but leaving faster
- Feature adoption stalling — a feature they previously used regularly has gone quiet
- Number of active users inside the account shrinking

**Relationship signals**
- Champion goes quiet — stops responding to emails, cancels check-in calls
- Stakeholder change — champion leaves the company or changes role
- Escalating support tickets — more issues raised, or the same issue raised repeatedly
- Response times lengthening — they used to reply same day, now it takes a week

**Commercial signals**
- Missed or delayed renewal conversations
- Pushback on upsell conversations they previously seemed open to
- Requests to pause, reduce, or restructure the contract

When you see two or more of these signals in the same account, open this playbook.

## Step 1 — Pull the account history

Before the call, you need a clear picture of what actually happened, based on real data.

You are looking for four things:

- **Usage trend** over the last 90 days — is engagement declining, flat, or did it drop suddenly at a specific point?
- **The trigger moment** — when exactly did things change? Was it gradual or was there a clear before and after?
- **Stakeholder changes** — did their champion leave? Did a new decision-maker join who never experienced the onboarding?
- **Unresolved friction** — open support tickets, bugs reported, features requested and never delivered, onboarding steps never completed

| **Salesforce** | Contact history, stakeholder changes, open cases, activity timeline |
| **Gong / Chorus** | Review the last 2-3 recorded calls — what did the customer say that you may have underweighted? |
| **Intercom / Zendesk** | Support ticket volume and sentiment trend over the last 90 days |

**For early-stage companies without CS tooling (under 100 employees)**

You do not need Gainsight to do this well. You need discipline.

- **Product database or admin panel** — log in and look at last login dates, active users, and feature usage directly
- **Your CRM notes** (HubSpot, Pipedrive, or even a shared Notion doc) — read every interaction note from the last 6 months
- **Email thread history** -how often were they reaching out? Did frequency drop? Did their response times get longer?
- **A simple spreadsheet** —build a 90-day timeline yourself. Date, event, signal. You will see the pattern.

## Step 2 — Build your hypothesis

Before the call you need one specific, evidence-based hypothesis that answers this question:

**What happened to this customer, and when did it start?**

There are three root causes. Every churn maps to one of them.

| Root cause | What the data looks like | What it means for the call |
|------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Product gap** | Good early adoption, then drop after a specific feature gap or product update | Acknowledge the gap directly. Do not defend it. |
| **Relationship break** | Usage was fine until a stakeholder change — champion left, new buyer came in cold | The new stakeholder never chose you. Restart the relationship from scratch. |
| **Wrong fit from the start** | Low activation from day one, usage never built momentum | The hardest conversation. Onboarding or sales set unrealistic expectations. |

Write your hypothesis in one sentence before the call. If you cannot write it in one sentence, you do not have a hypothesis yet — you have a guess.

---

## Step 3 — Prepare your questions

Your hypothesis determines your questions. You are not going into this call to ask the customer to diagnose their own problem. You are going in with a point of view and using questions to confirm or challenge it.

The difference between a weak question and a strong one:

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| "What went wrong for you?" | "We noticed engagement dropped in October — was that around when things started to feel difficult?" |
| "Are you happy with the product?" | "When your team lead changed in September, did the new team get a proper onboarding on the platform?" |
| "What would it take for you to stay?" | "The two features you raised in July were not delivered — how much of your frustration comes from that specifically?" |

Strong questions show you already know the shape of the problem. That alone changes how the customer sees you.

**Prepare three questions maximum.** More than three and you are fishing, not diagnosing.

<summary><strong>Tools for this step</strong> &nbsp;(click to expand)</summary>

<br>

**For companies with CS tooling**

Use **Gong or Chorus** to review how your past calls with this customer went. Where did they express frustration? What did they ask for that never happened? Your questions should reference those moments specifically.

**For early-stage companies without CS tooling**

Use **Notion or a simple document** to build a pre-call prep template you fill in every time. Three sections: hypothesis, three questions, what I will acknowledge first.

**Loom** is underrated here — record a 3-minute private video walking yourself through the account before the call. It forces you to organise your thinking out loud.

## Step 4 — The call itself

**Open with acknowledgement.** Not "we really value your business." Be specific to what this customer went through.

*"I want to start by acknowledging that the last few months have not been what we promised. I know that has been frustrating for your team and I am not here to defend it."*

**Then show your evidence.**

*"I pulled your usage data before this call. I can see engagement dropped in October, right around when your team lead changed. I think I understand what happened and I want to talk through it with you."*

**Then ask your prepared questions.** Allow them to respond and then move to the next. Do not rush to solutions yet.

**What not to do:**

- Do not open with a discount it signals panic and devalues your product
- Do not defend your team, your product, or your company in the first half of the call
- Do not bring a roadmap slide to a first churn call it reads as deflection
- Do not ask "what would it take for you to stay?" in the first five minutes it skips the acknowledgement they need

## The pre-call checklist

Use this before you join the call.

- [ ] Pulled usage data for the last 90 days and identified when engagement dropped
- [ ] Identified the trigger moment what changed and when
- [ ] Checked for stakeholder changes in the account in the last 6 months
- [ ] Reviewed open support tickets and unresolved friction points
- [ ] Written one hypothesis sentence: what happened and when it started
- [ ] Prepared three specific questions that test the hypothesis
- [ ] Decided what I will acknowledge first specific, not generic
- [ ] Reminded myself: acknowledge before I question, evidence before I solve

---

## Quick reference

```
Before the call:   Data → Hypothesis → Questions → What to acknowledge
On the call:       Acknowledge → Evidence → Questions → Listen
Never:             Discount first / Defend first / Roadmap first
```
